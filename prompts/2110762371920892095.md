## Prompt (English)

```text
Create a high-end cinematic and educational 3D animation in Three.js explaining how a Tesla-style electric motor works. The animation must have an EXACT FIXED DURATION OF 40 SECONDS. Do not make the animation shorter or longer than 40 seconds. Final output: Exact duration: 40.0 seconds Resolution: 1920x1080 Aspect ratio: 16:9 Frame rate: 30 FPS Total frames: 1200 High-quality anti-aliasing High-resolution textures Smooth deterministic animation Extremely realistic visual quality The overall visual quality should resemble a premium automotive engineering visualization or a high-end technical product commercial. Build a detailed electric motor assembly inspired by the permanent-magnet AC motors used in modern Tesla vehicles. Do not use Tesla logos or branding. FIXED TIMELINE 0–5 seconds — Hero introduction Show the fully assembled electric motor suspended in a dark premium studio environment. Use: cinematic lighting realistic brushed aluminum copper steel magnets insulation bearings realistic reflections ambient occlusion realistic shadows subtle depth of field Use a slow cinematic orbit around the motor. During the final part of this section, make the outer housing partially transparent to reveal the internal structure. 5–11 seconds — Internal components Move the camera closer and reveal the internal motor components. Clearly show: stator copper windings rotor permanent magnets motor shaft bearings housing cooling channels electrical connections Highlight the important components one at a time. Use floating labels with short technical names. Keep non-highlighted components slightly darker or semi-transparent. 11–19 seconds — Exploded view Create a dramatic and highly detailed exploded-view animation. Separate the motor into many individual mechanical components along the motor axis. Include: outer housing front cover rear cover bearings shaft rotor core rotor magnets stator core copper windings cooling components seals electrical connectors fasteners Keep every component perfectly aligned. The viewer must clearly understand where every piece belongs. Move the camera slightly through the exploded assembly while highlighting the major components. Near the end of this section, smoothly begin reassembling the motor. 19–28 seconds — Electromagnetic operation Finish reassembling the motor. Explain how electricity creates rotation. Visualize three-phase AC current flowing through the stator windings. Use animated electrical pulses inside the copper coils. Visualize the rotating magnetic field around the stator using elegant semi-transparent magnetic field lines, arrows, or vector effects. Show how the rotating magnetic field interacts with the permanent magnets inside the rotor. Clearly visualize magnetic attraction and repulsion. The rotor should begin rotating slowly and gradually accelerate. Display these short explanations: "Three-phase current creates a rotating magnetic field." "The magnetic field interacts with the rotor." "This interaction generates torque." 28–34 seconds — Technical close-up and power flow Show a close-up cross-section of the stator and rotor. Visualize: magnetic north and south poles rotating stator magnetic field rotor magnetic field electric current direction torque direction rotor rotation direction Optionally show three small animated sine waves representing phases A, B, and C, separated by 120 degrees. Then visualize the complete energy conversion process: Electrical energy → stator current → rotating magnetic field → electromagnetic torque → rotor rotation → mechanical output through the shaft. Use clean animated arrows to show the energy flow. 34–38 seconds — High-speed operation Show the motor operating at high RPM. The rotor should spin smoothly while the stator remains stationary. Add subtle motion blur. Show cooling flow through the housing using translucent fluid paths. Visualize heat being removed from the stator and copper windings. 38–40 seconds — Final hero shot Transition back to the fully assembled motor. Show a slow cinematic rotation. Briefly use a transparent cutaway so the spinning rotor can still be seen inside. Finish with the text: "Electrical energy → Electromagnetic torque → Mechanical rotation" The animation must end exactly at 40.0 seconds. VISUAL QUALITY The animation should be: photorealistic cinematic technically believable futuristic premium educational clean and uncluttered Avoid cartoon styling. Use realistic: machining details bolts screws copper winding geometry segmented magnets bearing geometry cooling channels metallic surfaces manufacturing details Use physically based materials with: metallic properties roughness variation normal maps realistic reflections subtle surface imperfections CAMERA Use only smooth cinematic camera movement: slow orbit shots macro close-ups controlled dolly-ins exploded-view fly-throughs cross-section shots internal motor views Avoid: shaky camera movement sudden cuts excessive zooming unrealistic camera acceleration ANIMATION REQUIREMENTS All transitions must be smooth and deliberate. The exploded-view sequence should be one of the main visual highlights. All mechanical components must separate and reassemble with precise motion. Electrical currents, magnetic fields, torque indicators, and rotor rotation must remain synchronized. The entire animation must use the same fixed 40-second timeline on every execution so the prompt can be used as a repeatable benchmark between different AI coding and 3D generation systems.
```
